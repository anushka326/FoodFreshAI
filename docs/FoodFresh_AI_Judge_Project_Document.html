1. Executive summary

FoodFresh AI is a prototype food-management platform that combines image-based food identification, visual freshness classification, reference-based shelf-life estimates, pantry history and consumption-priority guidance. Its product goal is to help a household notice food it already has and decide what to use first.

The problem is practical: groceries are stored in multiple places, conditions differ by food, and people may forget what they bought. A date reminder or inventory list can record an item, but it does not necessarily connect its visible condition and storage context to a next step. FoodFresh AI brings those pieces into one workflow.

The current backend runs a hybrid vision pipeline using Grounding DINO for open-vocabulary object detection, SigLIP 2 for image/text semantic matching, and a ResNet-50 raw-food specialist. The fused food identity is passed to an AgriFreshNET fine-tuned ResNet-18 freshness model and a deterministic USDA FoodKeeper reference/estimation service. Pantry records and chat history persist in SQLite; an explicit Eat First rule engine ranks saved items. FreshoBuddy provides a conversational interface with structured pantry answers handled deterministically and other food questions eligible for a backend Gemini response when configured.

Interpretation boundary. Image analysis estimates visible surface condition. It cannot inspect internal contamination, measure microbes, smell food, or certify safety. FoodKeeper durations and remaining-life values are estimates that depend on the correctness of food identity, dates and storage context.
Food image→Detect and identify→Visible freshness→FoodKeeper estimate→Pantry and Eat First→User action

FoodFresh AI is differentiated by this system-level connection, rather than by claiming a new neural architecture. Its present contribution is turning multiple predictions and reference rules into a persistent household workflow. Recipes from pantry ingredients, NGO donation, impact measurement and a full PWA remain future scope.

2. Problem statement

Households often lack a reliable, low-effort way to track food identity, storage context, visible deterioration and intended use together. Items can be forgotten in a refrigerator or pantry; foods differ in storage guidance; and appearance is not always easy to assess consistently. Static expiry reminders do not observe current appearance, while a food classifier by itself does not tell a household what to do next.

This project addresses the design problem: How can an accessible digital system combine a food photograph, user-provided storage context and a household pantry record to support more timely food-use decisions without overstating what computer vision can know?

The social importance is substantial, but the project makes no claim that the prototype has already reduced waste. UNEP's 2024 report estimates 1.05 billion tonnes of food waste at retail, food-service and household levels in 2022; households contributed about 60% of that total [8]. The estimate motivates prevention tools; it is not an impact metric for this project.

Food waste prevention can conserve the land, water, energy and labor used to produce food. FoodFresh AI targets one small point in that chain: the household decision between use soon, store appropriately, or seek another safe and practical action.

3. Proposed solution

Observe → Understand → Predict → Recommend → Act

  1. Observe: the user submits a food image and storage information.
  2. Understand: computer-vision components propose an identity; a fusion step resolves model evidence and may return uncertainty.
  3. Predict: the freshness classifier estimates a visible class, while FoodKeeper rules estimate a storage-life window for a canonical food/storage match.
  4. Recommend: the application combines that window, freshness and saved pantry state to prioritize items.
  5. Act: the user can inspect the result, save it to pantry history and ask FreshoBuddy for guidance.

Prediction is a model or rule output. Recommendation is a ranked next step, such as use soon. Action is something the user actually does—cook, store, share or discard. The prototype supports analysis and prioritization; it does not yet complete meal generation or donation transactions.

Example future workflow: “Spinach appears to have a short estimated quality window → use it soon → show recipes based on spinach, tomato and onion → let the user choose a meal.” The recipe step is proposed future work; it is not represented as an implemented pantry-aware recipe feature.

5. Project objectives

Technical objectives

  • Identify common food items from images while exposing uncertainty when evidence conflicts.
  • Estimate visible freshness condition using a trained image classifier.
  • Combine a food identity with reference storage guidance and user context to estimate a quality window.
  • Maintain per-user pantry records and history in persistent storage.
  • Rank saved items with explainable, deterministic urgency rules.
  • Provide natural-language support without allowing generated text to overwrite structured pantry values.
  • Evaluate models on held-out data and representative image fixtures, keeping dataset and external model-card scores clearly separated.

Social and environmental objectives

  • Encourage people to notice and use food they have already purchased.
  • Promote more deliberate storage and household inventory habits.
  • Build a future pathway from “use soon” to recipe or verified food-rescue options.
  • Define impact measures before making a waste-reduction claim.

9. Machine-learning concepts

Classification learns a mapping from input to a finite set of labels. Multi-class classification selects among more than two labels. Freshness uses Fresh, Rotten and Slightly Spoiled; food recognition proposes an identity from its own vocabulary. These tasks are distinct: knowing an item is a tomato does not determine its freshness.

Pretraining supplies general visual features learned from other data. Fine-tuning updates some or all of those parameters on the target dataset. Augmentation—such as modest rotation or horizontal flip—can expose a model to plausible input changes. It cannot create missing food classes or real storage histories.

Data are separated into training (fit parameters), validation (select settings/checkpoints), and test (final held-out measurement). Leakage occurs when near-duplicate or related source images cross splits, making scores too optimistic. The local AgriFreshNET manifest was checked for shared base stems across splits.

MetricMeaningWhy it matters
AccuracyCorrect predictions divided by all examples.Easy summary, but can hide poor minority-class performance.
PrecisionOf predictions for a class, the share correct.Shows false alarms for that class.
RecallOf actual examples in a class, the share found.Shows misses for that class.
F1-scoreHarmonic mean of precision and recall.Summarizes trade-off; macro F1 weights classes equally.
Confusion matrixCounts actual versus predicted classes.Shows which freshness stages are confused.
ECE / NLLCalibration/error measures for probability confidence.Help assess confidence quality; do not prove safety or generalization.

Domain shift occurs when deployment images differ from training data in food varieties, lighting, background, camera, occlusion or spoilage. A model can score strongly on a held-out split from the same collection process and still fail on unfamiliar phone images. The project’s 11 selected fixtures illustrate why accuracy must be measured on broader, independent data; they are not a representative benchmark.

11. Data cleaning and preprocessing

  • Manifest rows are organized with food identity, freshness label, image path and the source folder’s interval annotation.
  • All local manifest paths were verified to resolve; the train, validation and test split totals are 9,856, 2,131 and 2,173.
  • Grouping by base image stem found no stem crossing between splits. This reduces one form of near-duplicate leakage; it does not prove independence across every capture session or source.
  • Image decoding applies EXIF orientation correction and RGB conversion. Freshness evaluation uses the training-compatible resize/crop and ImageNet normalization.
  • Freshness V3’s evaluation transform is resize to 256, center crop to 224, tensor conversion and ImageNet normalization. Training uses limited image augmentation as specified in the trainer.
  • The classifier is evaluated on the test manifest after model/checkpoint selection on validation data.

The local AgriFreshNET analysis found 14,160 images distributed evenly among freshness stages, with 590 samples in each food-stage folder. Equal counts are useful for controlled comparison, but do not represent real-world prevalence. Images with different lighting, occlusion, packaging or multiple items can still produce difficult predictions.

13. Hybrid AI architecture

React image form→FastAPI validation and image decode→Grounding DINO regions
↓ candidate crop / image context
Raw-food ResNet-50+SigLIP 2 image/text scores→Evidence fusion and uncertainty→Food identity
↓ parallel downstream assessment
Freshness V2+FoodKeeper reference + storage/days→Analysis result→SQLite pantry/history
↓ persistent pantry context
Deterministic Eat First→FreshoBuddy / user decision

The pipeline is modular: detector output provides regions; the semantic and specialist classifiers provide complementary identity evidence; deterministic fusion produces an identity or uncertainty; freshness and shelf-life components provide separate outputs. A food identity match is still fallible, and downstream values inherit that uncertainty.

REST endpoints connect the browser and backend. The health endpoint reports the active freshness checkpoint and calibration configuration. Research candidates remain outside production routing.

14. Database — SQLite

SQLite is appropriate for a local demonstration and a single backend instance: it is embedded, requires no separate database server, and makes persistence easy to inspect and back up. It is not assumed to be the final choice for high concurrency or multi-instance deployment.

The verified database contains seven tables: users, auth_sessions, pantry_history, pantry_meta, conversations, messages and ml_feedback. Pantry fields record analysis identifiers, food/form, freshness, estimate inputs/results, storage information and model-version provenance. Conversations and messages are user-scoped.

At the last read-only verification for this document, the database held 37 pantry records, 6 users, 12 conversations, 34 messages and 0 feedback records. These are development-instance counts, not product adoption statistics. No user database file is required in a public source checkout.

15. FreshoBuddy AI

FreshoBuddy is the conversational food companion. It can respond to household questions using saved pantry context and can provide general food-management guidance. The service loads its Gemini key on the backend; React does not need to receive the provider secret.

For “Which food should I eat first?” and a follow-up “Why?”, the implementation uses deterministic pantry triage. It computes from the application’s records and explains the saved remaining-day/urgency values. This avoids asking a generative model to recalculate structured pantry facts. Other intents can use Gemini with contextual data when configured; generated text is not an authoritative food-safety inspection.

Conversation records and messages persist in SQLite and are scoped to the authenticated user. If external model access is unavailable, the experience depends on the implemented deterministic/fallback behavior. Exact availability depends on local secret configuration and network conditions.

16. Eat First system

Eat First is a four-tier deterministic priority engine. It reads the FoodKeeper remaining estimate and the freshness label. It does not train on user history, forecast future spoilage, or use a neural ranking model.

TierRule outlineExample explanation
VERY_HIGHRotten label, no estimated days left, or an estimated window at or below about one day.Quality estimate is expired/critically short; a rotten visible label triggers caution.
HIGHSlightly spoiled or estimated average/maximum window at or below about three days.Use soon because the estimated window is short.
MEDIUMEstimated remaining window at or below about seven days.Prioritize within the week.
LOWLonger estimate with fresh visible class.Ample estimated window remains.

Ties are handled as ties; the assistant may explain both items. If the FoodKeeper match is unavailable, the system returns unavailable guidance instead of inventing a duration. The priority is a decision aid, not an instruction to eat food that smells, feels or appears unsafe.

17. Current system limitations

  • Image dependence: blur, glare, shadows, distance, occlusion, mixed items and unusual crops can change predictions.
  • Visible-condition boundary: an RGB image cannot detect internal deterioration, microbes, allergens, toxins or unsafe temperature history.
  • Class coverage: the local AgriFreshNET freshness subset includes eight foods. Broader pretrained identity models do not make the freshness classifier universal. Chilli coverage is especially limited in freshness training; bread is outside the AgriFreshNET classes.
  • Food identity errors: the FoodKeeper lookup depends on a correct canonical match. Ambiguous identity can make shelf-life estimates unavailable or misleading.
  • Reference-window uncertainty: FoodKeeper guidance is general; actual freshness depends on initial quality, package, ripeness, time and temperature history.
  • Dataset shift: same-source held-out data is not equivalent to new households, cameras, regions or produce varieties.
  • Freshness V3 discrepancy: trainer-reported metrics do not match independently reproduced checkpoint evaluation. V3 was not promoted.
  • Shelf-life ground truth: taxonomy intervals are not measured per-image remaining-life observations. The interval candidate does not estimate day errors.
  • Evaluation: the 11-image smoke set is small and selected; no broad external clinical, microbiological or prospective household trial has been performed.
  • Prototype operations: SQLite is single-instance oriented, Gemini relies on external configuration/connectivity, and no verified NGO or PWA rollout exists.
Food safety: A “Fresh” prediction is not a safety guarantee. Users should follow applicable food-safety guidance and their own sensory checks; discard food if it appears unsafe.

19. Social and environmental impact

Food waste has economic, social and environmental consequences. UNEP estimates that 1.05 billion tonnes were wasted at consumer-facing stages in 2022, including inedible parts; 60% was attributed to households [8]. FoodFresh AI aligns with the intent of SDG target 12.3 to reduce food waste, but this prototype has not measured household behavior or avoided waste.

The hypothesized pathway is:

Better awareness → better timing decisions → more food used or redirected → less avoidable waste → better use of production resources

Each arrow requires evidence. A dashboard may show an estimate; a credible impact evaluation needs a baseline, documented actions and preferably measured quantities. A predicted “saved” item is not necessarily eaten, donated or otherwise kept out of waste.

22. Scalability

  • Models: broaden produce classes using licensed, regionally representative data and evaluate each new class outside the training source.
  • Inference: benchmark CPU/GPU options, use model-serving processes with explicit resource limits, and separate long-running inference from interactive web processes.
  • API: package backend components consistently, add health/latency/error monitoring and scale stateless API workers after moving state out of one local file.
  • Database: retain SQLite for small local demos; migrate to managed PostgreSQL when concurrency, multiple application replicas, backup or transactional needs exceed a single-file model.
  • Images: use object storage with retention controls rather than putting image binaries into relational tables; collect only what is needed.
  • Product: add multilingual names, regional FoodKeeper mapping, preferences and verified food-rescue partners only after data and operating rules are established.

23. Security and privacy

  • Passwords are stored as salted PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 hashes; authentication uses expiring bearer sessions.
  • Routes resolve the user from the session and scope pantry/conversation records to that user. Access isolation should remain part of regression testing.
  • Gemini credentials are read by backend code from environment configuration and must never be committed or exposed in frontend bundles.
  • Images and pantry data can reveal household routines. A deployment needs a clear retention/deletion policy, consent for model feedback and secure backups.
  • SQLite database files, raw datasets, model checkpoints, environment files and large archives are excluded from the source push; users should create their own local data/configuration.
  • No security certification, independent penetration test or formal privacy audit is claimed.

24. Project learning

  • Technical: image classification/detection, transfer learning, preprocessing consistency, REST integration, database persistence and full-stack request flows.
  • Research: dataset provenance, split leakage checks, class coverage, calibration, model uncertainty and the difference between dataset test accuracy and real-world behavior.
  • System design: multiple models need an explicit fusion policy; reference rules must not be mislabeled as learned models; generated language should not overrule structured user records.
  • Product: a prediction becomes useful only when it supports an understandable next step. That action still depends on user judgment and reliable system context.
  • Responsible use: visible appearance is not food safety, taxonomy intervals are not observed days, and external model-card performance is not a project benchmark.

25. Project contribution

FoodFresh AI’s contribution is the integration of food recognition, visible freshness estimation, reference-based shelf-life guidance, persistent pantry history, deterministic urgency and conversational support in a single prototype. The work is an applied system and workflow contribution, not a newly invented CNN or a validated food-safety technology.

  • Connects visual analysis to storage guidance and pantry records.
  • Exposes an explainable Eat First tier system.
  • Preserves saved context across sessions for follow-up assistance.
  • Documents evaluation limits and refuses to promote a weaker/unreconciled model candidate.
  • Defines recipes, PWA support, donation and measurable impact as future work.

26. If a judge remembers only eight things

  1. Food waste is a household problem as well as a supply-chain issue; this project focuses on household decisions.
  2. FoodFresh AI combines image analysis, storage references and saved pantry context in one workflow.
  3. Recognition, freshness and shelf-life are distinct components with distinct limits.
  4. Freshness output describes visible appearance, not certified safety.
  5. Eat First is explainable deterministic prioritization, not a learned ranking model.
  6. FreshoBuddy grounds “eat first” answers in pantry records instead of inventing pantry facts.
  7. Pantry-aware recipes and NGO donation are future scope; no partnership or waste impact is claimed.
  8. Model metrics are tied to named splits; real-world fixtures show errors and the V3 discrepancy remains disclosed.

Food monitoring

Food intelligence
↓
Action guidance
↓
Measured food-waste prevention

The sequence is a roadmap, not the current deployment state. Each step should follow data quality, usability and safety validation. A donation network in particular depends on real partner organizations and operational responsibility.

28. Main motto

Know Your Food.

Understand what you have and what its visible condition suggests.

Save Your Food.

Use storage context and priority guidance to notice food before its estimated quality window closes.

Waste Less.

Consume or repurpose usable food—and, in a future verified network, redirect suitable surplus.

FoodFresh AI is not only about detecting food — it is about helping people decide what to do with it.
